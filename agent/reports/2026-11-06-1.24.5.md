## What the small models' rows said

The 35B passes nearly everything, so its runs had stopped saying where
Lantern is weak. The 2B's 59 failed rows and the 0.8B's 93 said it at once,
and most of it was not about what the models know:

- they copy what the instructions show them: the example of a NEED, word
  for word, for four unrelated questions; `NEED:` and then the question,
  because the instructions said `NEED: <question>`; the example sum's
  `/1000` where the question was already in kilowatts;
- they were given 75 functions and 9,000 characters of instructions for
  "How do I make river water safe to drink?";
- Lantern read strictly what it could have read plainly (`ANS: 120*80/10000`,
  `= MAKE WHEEL:`), and let a conversation outgrow the model with no way
  back;
- and Lantern did not use what it knew: it had found the 2B's eight
  sentences in the lesson, and reported that none was backed.

Each is a change in 1.24.5. None is a bench answer: the tests replay the
rows through the changed code, and where Lantern now sends something back
the reply that returns is written by hand, so **what the 2B and the 0.8B
actually do with 1.24.5 is not known until their run on it is read**.
